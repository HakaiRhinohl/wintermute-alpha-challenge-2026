# Ascend: A Launchpad That Buys the Ecosystem

*Every successful launch can create demand for HYPE, KNTQ, and the tokens built around them.*

Most launchpads have a familiar economic destination.

Projects launch, trading begins, fees accrue, and a portion of those fees eventually supports the launchpad's own token. The platform can win even when the assets that generated its activity lose liquidity and attention.

Ascend is proposing the inverse model for Elysium.

It will not issue a protocol token. Instead, its economic design directs the value created by launched projects back into the surrounding ecosystem: HYPE, KNTQ, qualifying ecosystem tokens, builders, and users.

## A launchpad without another token 

Issuing a platform token creates a structural conflict for a launchpad. The protocol needs its launches to remain liquid, but it also has a separate liquid asset that needs incentives, volume, and buybacks of its own.

Ascend removes that second destination. There is no ASCEND token to absorb the protocol's revenue, Ascend is designed to become a recurring buyer of assets relevant to Elysium.

After creator, operator, or deployer payments, Ascend allocates **90% of net protocol revenue to HYPE purchases** and **10% to KNTQ purchases**.

For launched tokens, Ascend proposes a 1% trading fee. Depending on the launch arrangement, 25% or 50% of that fee goes first to the deployer, an approved active creator, or an approved takeover operator. Separate swap fees may also apply, and their remaining protocol share enters the same allocation framework.

## Where the HYPE purchases go

Buying HYPE is only the first step. Ascend proposes to divide the HYPE it acquires from protocol revenue four ways:

| Destination | Share of purchased HYPE | Function |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Protocol-owned HYPE staking | 40% | Builds a staked position whose yield buys ecosystem tokens to burn |
| Ecosystem-token purchases | 30% | Accumulates qualifying Ascended tokens in treasury; a small portion is burned |
| Treasury and operations | 20% | Funds development, infrastructure, team, and protocol expenses |
| Direct HYPE rewards | 10% | Distributed to eligible Ascend stakers |

This is the core loop in a compact form:

```text
Trading activity
      ↓
Net Ascend revenue
      ↓
90% HYPE purchases ──→ protocol-owned staked HYPE ──→ yield
      │                                                  ↓
      ├──→ treasury purchases of qualifying tokens       ecosystem-token buys and burns
      └──→ direct HYPE rewards for eligible stakers

10% KNTQ purchases
```

The design creates a protocol balance sheet rather than a one-off buyback headline. Forty percent of purchased HYPE is intended to stay staked. Its yield is then used to buy qualifying ecosystem tokens, which are burned. The HYPE principal remains staked, while its yield becomes a recurring source of demand for the ecosystem.

The separate 30% allocation has a different job. It acquires qualifying project tokens for Ascend's treasury, with a small unspecified portion burned. Those treasury positions may later help support eligible projects moving toward a HyperCore listing. They are not the pool from which stakers receive token airdrops.

That separation is easy to miss, and it makes the proposal more coherent. One mechanism creates a recurring burn engine; another builds a treasury capable of supporting projects beyond their first days of trading.

## HYPE holders get ecosystem exposure through yield, not principal

Ascend's staking product adds a second, distinct loop.

Under the proposed model, users deposit HYPE, which is converted to kHYPE (which also funds KNTQ buybacks). The yield generated by a user's kHYPE position buys qualifying ecosystem tokens, and those tokens are distributed to stakers in proportion to eligible HYPE stake.

```text
User HYPE → kHYPE → staking yield → ecosystem-token purchases → staker distributions
```
That allows a HYPE holder to retain underlying HYPE exposure while directing the yield toward early ecosystem assets. 
Protocol fees create a separate HYPE reward stream: 10% of Ascend's purchased HYPE is allocated to direct rewards for eligible stakers. Ascend NFTs may increase a user's weight in that HYPE reward pool, but not in the ecosystem-token distributions funded by kHYPE yield.

## KNTQ has two routes to demand

KNTQ is not merely adjacent to the model.

First, Ascend's own allocation sends 10% of net protocol revenue to KNTQ purchases. 

Second, Ascend runs on Elysium. Kinetiq's stated Elysium model directs 50% of sequencer revenue to programmatic open-market KNTQ purchases, with tokens sent to the Hyperliquid Assistance Fund for permanent removal from supply.

So one active Ascend market can feed KNTQ at two layers:

```text
Ascend's net revenue     → direct KNTQ purchases
Ascend's Elysium activity → sequencer revenue → KNTQ purchases and removal
+
Structural kHYPE demand
```

The first depends on Ascend's fee capture. The second depends on the blockspace and sequencer revenue that Ascend activity generates on Elysium. Together they make Ascend a potentially contributor to KNTQ's economic model.

## The real product is the lifecycle

The strongest part of the thesis is not the buyback schedule. It is the lifecycle behind it.

Ascend is trying to make a launch the beginning of a project relationship rather than the point at which a platform moves on to the next ticker. Its proposed route is:

```text
Launch → continued trading and visibility → Ascended status
       → treasury / liquidity support → HyperCore spot → eligible HIP-3 perps
```

The platform's incentives are aligned with that path. More durable activity produces creator payments and net protocol revenue. That revenue buys HYPE and KNTQ, accumulates qualifying ecosystem tokens and supports the protocol's HYPE staking position.

## The Ascend thesis

Most launchpads ask the ecosystem to create value for the launchpad token.

Ascend is trying to make the launchpad a buyer of the ecosystem that creates its revenue.

If the model works,it can contribute to HYPE demand and staking, direct KNTQ purchases, Elysium sequencer revenue, ecosystem support, and recurring token burns..

That is a more ambitious standard than simply launching the next token. It turns the launchpad into infrastructure with an ecosystem balance sheet and makes the quality and durability of the projects it launches matter to the protocol itself.
